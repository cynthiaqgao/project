1 Establish the data fit

I opened the public Airbnb listings file for the Los Angeles market, stored in the workspace as listings.csv, from the Inside Airbnb public data archive: https://insideairbnb.com/get-the-data/.

Source and scope. The file contains one row per Airbnb listing. The sample has 43,932 rows and 19 columns, covering residential and short-stay listings across Los Angeles-area neighborhoods such as Hollywood, Venice, Santa Monica, Downtown, Long Beach, Pasadena, Beverly Hills, and Glendale. This is a cross-sectional snapshot, not a time-series dataset, so it supports market comparison rather than a dated trend analysis.

Column and source table What it contains How we will use it
neighbourhood in listings Neighborhood name for each listing Filter by area and compare local pricing and demand.
room_type in listings Entire home/apartment, private room, hotel room, or shared room Compare market segments and evaluate booking-type tradeoffs.
price in listings Nightly price in USD Main affordability metric and central filter for decision support.
availability_365 in listings Number of nights available over the next 365 days Proxy for occupancy and supply; spot high-demand areas.
number_of_reviews in listings Total review count Measure proof of market traction and listing activity.
reviews_per_month in listings Average monthly review activity Estimate recent demand and recency of guest interest.
latitude and longitude in listings Geographic location for each listing Show a map of density and pricing patterns by area.
minimum_nights in listings Minimum stay requirement Understand booking flexibility and host expectations.

What the data can support. The available data supports a decision dashboard for either travelers or hosts. It can answer questions such as which neighborhoods offer the best balance of price, availability, and review activity, which room types are most competitive, and where supply may be more constrained. The dashboard could support neighborhood and room-type filters, a price-vs-availability scatterplot, average price by area, a ranked listing table, and a map showing listing density and average nightly rate.

Important missing information includes seasonal demand, amenities, host response time, cancellation policy, and listing photos. Those gaps are manageable: a smaller initial scope can focus on price, neighborhood, room type, availability, and review volume. A second phase could add amenities and calendar data from a separate source to improve conversion and recommendation quality.
